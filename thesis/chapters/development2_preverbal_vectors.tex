\chapter{Pre-Verbal Affective Vector Projection}
\label{chap:development2_preverbal_vectors}

The previous chapter established that, for a given target emotion $e$, the decomposed steering vector
\[
\Delta_e(\alpha_G,\alpha_R)=\alpha_G\mathbf{g}+\alpha_R\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e
\]
reliably shifts the affective framing of generated text towards $e$ while preserving semantic content.
In that framework, joy, sadness, anger, and the other Plutchik categories were treated as \textbf{primitive} units. This means that each $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ was taken to be an atomic emotion-specific vector without further decomposition.\par

This chapter re-examines that assumption. The central question is whether named emotion categories, such as \textbf{joy} or \textbf{fear}, are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the minimal units of affective representation in the model's residual stream, or
  \item whether each $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ is itself a composite that consists of a small set of more fundamental, pre-categorical affective dimensions.
\end{itemize}
The experiments reported here provide evidence for the latter. Namely, within each named emotion, there exist local sub-axes, and these sub-axes are largely shared across emotion categories. They can be interpreted as vectors that are more primitive than any named emotion label.\par

The central conclusion is that what Chapter~\ref{chap:development1_emo_steering} called $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ can be re-read as a projection onto a shared pre-verbal affective space:
\[
\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e
\;=\;
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\beta_{e,k}\,\mathbf{m}_k
\;+\;
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e,
\]
where $\mathbf{m}_k$ are label-free meta-axes shared across all emotions, $\beta_{e,k}=\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e^\top\mathbf{m}_k$ are scalar projections that encode the ``affective fingerprint'' of emotion $e$, and $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$ is the residual component that is orthogonal to all $\mathbf{m}_k$.\par

\section*{Experimental Roadmap}

This chapter is organised as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Internal variance structure}: We characterise the internal dimensionality of each named emotion by applying PCA to the distribution of per-source residual vectors within each emotion category.
  \item \textbf{Shared meta-axes via pooled PCA}: We show that a single per-emotion-centred pooled PCA directly recovers a set of shared meta-axes $\mathbf{m}_k$, without requiring separate per-emotion PCAs or sign alignment.
  \item \textbf{Pre-verbal interpretation}: We interpret each $\mathbf{m}_k$ by presenting extreme examples from all emotions simultaneously to an LLM, obtaining emotion-independent semantic labels for each meta-axis.
  \item \textbf{Re-decomposition of $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$}: We project each $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ onto the $\mathbf{m}_k$ basis, quantify the explained variance, and state the revised decomposition that extends the formula from Chapter~\ref{chap:development1_emo_steering}.
  \item \textbf{Causal validation by meta-axis steering}: We test whether adding $\pm\beta\,\mathbf{m}_1$ to the steering vector causally shifts outputs along the expected affective sub-dimension.
  \item \textbf{Label-free steering}: We compare three steering conditions (named emotion prototype only, $\mathbf{m}_k$-projection only, and their combination) to determine whether the $\mathbf{m}_k$ decomposition retains the practical utility of $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Internal Variance Structure of Named Emotions}
\label{sec:ch2-variance-structure}

Chapter~\ref{chap:development1_emo_steering} established that $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ is a valid emotion-specific signal. After residualising the shared emotionalisation component $\mathbf{g}$, the eight residual directions recover a Plutchik-like geometry (adjacent~--~opposite cosine gap growing from $0.010$ to $0.041$), and a linear probe achieves $93.8\%$ eight-class accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:decomposition_validity}).\par

However, $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ is merely the average of many individual per-source residual shifts $\boldsymbol{\delta}^{g\perp}_{n,e,i}$ for the same emotion. These shifts may cluster tightly around the average direction, or spread across several distinct directions. This section examines the shape of that within-emotion distribution: whether each named emotion is well described by a single prototype direction, or whether it occupies a broader, multi-dimensional region in residual space.\par

\subsection*{Protocol}

For each emotion $e$, we collect the per-source, per-intensity residuals $\boldsymbol{\delta}^{g\perp}_{n,e,i}$ at layer~13, apply PCA, and compute the participation ratio
\[
\mathrm{PR}_e
=\frac{\left(\sum_k \lambda_k\right)^2}{\sum_k \lambda_k^2},
\]
where $\lambda_k$ are the eigenvalues of the empirical covariance.
To establish a baseline, we shuffle the emotion labels to destroy emotion-specific structure and report $\mathrm{PR}_{\mathrm{shuffle}}$. A value $\mathrm{PR}_e < \mathrm{PR}_{\mathrm{shuffle}}$ indicates that the emotion's internal distribution is more concentrated than random, whereas $\mathrm{PR}_e > \mathrm{PR}_{\mathrm{shuffle}}$ indicates a more diffuse, multi-axis structure.\par

\section{Shared Meta-Axes via Pooled PCA}
\label{sec:ch2-meta-axes}

The participation ratio analysis in Section~\ref{sec:ch2-variance-structure} established that each emotion's residuals occupy a multi-dimensional region rather than a single vector direction. We now investigate whether those dimensions are emotion-specific or whether they recur across all emotion categories, which determines whether $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ is a truly atomic vector or a projection onto a shared affective subspace.\par

We write $\mathbf{u}_{e,k}$ for the $k$-th principal component of the residuals of emotion $e$ alone, so $\mathbf{u}_{e,1}$ is the leading within-emotion variance direction, $\mathbf{u}_{e,2}$ is the second, and so on. The associated eigenvalue $\lambda_{e,k}$ measures the residual variance of emotion $e$ along $\mathbf{u}_{e,k}$.\par

Performing PCA separately per emotion yields a set of local eigenvectors $\{\mathbf{u}_{e,k}\}_k$ that capture the internal variance structure of each emotion. However, these per-emotion PCAs are not directly comparable{}: the $k$-th eigenvector of \textit{joy} need not correspond to the $k$-th eigenvector of \textit{sadness}, since their ordering is determined independently within each emotion block.\par

Throughout this section, $\mathbf{u}_{e,k}$ denotes a local PC from a PCA fitted within a single emotion block, $\lambda_{e,k}$ denotes its associated eigenvalue, and $\tilde{\mathbf{m}}_k$ denotes a pooled PC obtained from a single PCA fitted to the stacked residual matrix.\par

\subsection{Intuition of a Pooled PCA}

Suppose a vector $\mathbf{v}$ is shared across all eight emotions, appearing as the leadi ng eigenvector of each emotion's covariance with eigenvalue $\lambda_{e,1}$ for each $e$. Its contribution to $\Sigma_{\mathrm{pool}}$ is
\[
\sum_{e} \lambda_{e,1}\,\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^\top
= \Bigl(\lambda_{\mathrm{joy},1} + \lambda_{\mathrm{sadness},1} + \lambda_{\mathrm{anger},1} + \cdots\Bigr)\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^\top.
\]
The scalar prefactor accumulates the eigenvalue from every emotion. Concretely, if each $\lambda_{e,1} = 10$, the shared direction $\mathbf{v}$ contributes $10 \times 8 = 80$ to the pooled spectrum.\par

By contrast, suppose a vector $\mathbf{w}$ is specific to one emotion (e.g.\ \textit{anger}):
$\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{anger},2} \approx \mathbf{w}$, while the other emotions have negligible variance along $\mathbf{w}$. Its pooled contribution is
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{anger},2}\,\mathbf{w}\mathbf{w}^\top,
\]
a single term. Even if $\lambda_{\mathrm{anger},2} = 10$, the emotion-specific direction contributes only $10 \times 1 = 10$, one-eighth the weight of the shared direction. The pooled eigenvalue of $\mathbf{w}$ is suppressed by the very absence of cross-emotion support.\par

This asymmetry makes a single pooled PCA a \textbf{detector of commonality}: shared axes are promoted to the top of the spectrum by linear accumulation, while emotion-specific axes are diluted into lower components. A single PCA applied to the pooled residuals therefore preferentially recovers directions of variance that are consistently supported across all emotions, without requiring separate per-emotion PCAs or sign alignment.\par

\subsection{Protocol}

For each emotion $e$ we collect the pre-processed residuals $\{\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(3)}_{n,e,i}\}_{n,i}$ obtained by the four-step pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item compute the activation shift $\boldsymbol{\delta}_{n,e,i} = \mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{emo}}_{n,e,i} - \mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{neu}}_{n}$
  \item project out the shared emotionalisation direction $\mathbf{g}$
  \item project out the per-emotion prototype $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$
  \item subtract the source-wise mean across emotions to remove topic and style confounds
\end{enumerate}

We then centre each emotion block, stack all blocks into a single $(N \cdot E,\, D)$ matrix, and apply PCA. The resulting components $\{\tilde{\mathbf{m}}_k\}$ are the candidate meta-axes.\par

\subsection{Results}

\subsubsection*{Explained Variance}
The top three pooled components account for $11.4\%$, $4.5\%$, and $3.1\%$ of the total pooled variance (cumulative $18.9\%$). The first component is markedly dominant (more than twice the variance of the second), indicating a single primary shared dimension.\par

\subsubsection*{Alignment with Meta-axes}
To verify that the pooled components capture genuine cross-emotion structure, we compare them to the sign-aligned-mean meta-axes $\mathbf{m}_k$, defined as the normalised average of the per-emotion $k$-th eigenvectors after sign correction. Here $\sigma_{e,k}\in\{+1,-1\}$ corrects the arbitrary sign of each eigenvector before averaging across emotions:
\[
\mathbf{m}_k
=\frac{\sum_{e\in E}\sigma_{e,k}\,\mathbf{u}_{e,k}}
      {\left\lVert\sum_{e\in E}\sigma_{e,k}\,\mathbf{u}_{e,k}\right\rVert_2}.
\]
The pooled components $\tilde{\mathbf{m}}_k$ agree with $\mathbf{m}_k$ axis-by-axis and near-perfectly for the leading three components (Table~\ref{tab:pooled_vs_meta}, Figure~\ref{fig:pooled_robustness}).\par

\subsubsection*{Geometric Properties of $\mathbf{m}_k$}
The three meta-axes satisfy two further properties that confirm they capture affective \emph{texture} rather than the coarser emotion-identity or emotionalisation signals. First, all $\mathbf{m}_k$ are nearly orthogonal to the shared emotionalisation direction $\mathbf{g}$ ($|\cos(\mathbf{m}_k,\mathbf{g})| < 0.02$ for all $k$). Second, they are nearly orthogonal to every per-emotion prototype $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ ($|\cos(\mathbf{m}_k,\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e)| < 0.09$ for all $k,e$). Together, these two properties ensure that the $\mathbf{m}_k$ basis is geometrically independent of both the global emotionalisation axis and the emotion-label directions, capturing the within-emotion variance that prototype averaging obscures.\par

Components $\tilde{\mathbf{m}}_4$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{m}}_5$ do not correspond to any stable meta-axis: their explained variance is small ($2.2\%$ and $1.9\%$), their mutual-capture rate with any candidate higher-order axis falls below $0.50$, and PC$_4$--PC$_5$ show cosines below $0.45$ against all five $\mathbf{m}_k$ (Figure~\ref{fig:pooled_mk_heatmap}). We therefore restrict all subsequent claims to the stable top-three meta-axes $\mathbf{m}_1$, $\mathbf{m}_2$, and $\mathbf{m}_3$.\par

\subsection{Robustness: Convergence of Two Independent Methods}
\label{sec:ch2-pooled-robustness}

The convergence reported in Table~\ref{tab:pooled_vs_meta} and Figure~\ref{fig:pooled_robustness} is the outcome of a deliberate robustness test; this subsection makes the design of that test explicit and states what the convergence implies.\par

The sign-aligned mean and pooled PCA represent fundamentally different extraction strategies. The sign-aligned mean partitions the data by emotion label first: it runs a separate PCA within each of the eight emotion categories, then sign-aligns and averages the results rank by rank, relying on the emotion partition at every stage. Pooled PCA, by contrast, centres each emotion block to remove its mean and then applies a single decomposition to the stacked residuals. The centering step uses emotion labels, but the PCA itself sees only an unlabelled matrix and never consults which observation belongs to which emotion. The two methods therefore encode independent structural assumptions about how shared directions should be discovered.\par

One natural concern is that the meta-axes could be an artefact of the sign-aligned-mean procedure, for instance that the sign-alignment step or the per-emotion partition imposes structure that would not otherwise be present. Pooled PCA is immune to both the sign-alignment choice and to rank-wise recombination; after block-centering it applies a single decomposition to the stacked matrix without consulting emotion identity during the eigendecomposition itself. Yet it recovers the same three directions, matching each $\mathbf{m}_k$ individually at the axis level and collectively at the subspace level (Table~\ref{tab:pooled_vs_meta}, Figures~\ref{fig:pooled_robustness}--\ref{fig:pooled_mk_heatmap}), ruling out those methodological choices as the source of $\mathbf{m}_1$, $\mathbf{m}_2$, and $\mathbf{m}_3$.\par

Two methods that share no internal steps converge on the same three affective axes, constituting the strongest available evidence that $\mathbf{m}_1$, $\mathbf{m}_2$, and $\mathbf{m}_3$ reflect genuine shared structure in the model's residual activations rather than an artefact of any particular methodological choice.\par

\section{Re-Interpreting $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ as a Pre-Verbal Projection}
\label{sec:ch2-reinterpretation}

% THE KEY SECTION.
% r_e = Σ_k β_{e,k} m_k + ε_e
% β_{e,k} = r_e · m_k, explains ~22% of r_e on average.
% Named emotions are patterns in pre-verbal space.

The experiments so far establish that 
\begin{enumerate}
  \item each named emotion has internal sub-axes
  \item these sub-axes are shared across emotion categories
  \item the shared meta-axes have pre-verbal, emotion-independent interpretations
\end{enumerate}
This section draws these threads together into a revised decomposition of $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$.\par

\subsection*{Decomposition}

Each meta-axis $\mathbf{m}_k$ defines a direction in residual space. For a given emotion $e$, we define the \textbf{projection coefficient}
\[
\beta_{e,k} = \hat{\mathbf{r}}_e^\top\mathbf{m}_k
\]
and write the decomposition
\[
\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e
= \underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\beta_{e,k}\,\mathbf{m}_k}_{\text{pre-verbal projection}}
+ \underbrace{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e}_{\text{emotion-specific residual}},
\]
where $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e \perp \mathbf{m}_k$ for all $k$, and $K=5$ (all extracted meta-axes, since $\mathbf{m}_1$--$\mathbf{m}_3$ are stable while $\mathbf{m}_4$, $\mathbf{m}_5$ have lower method agreement). The explained variance ratio is $\mathrm{EVR}(e) = \sum_{k=1}^{K}\beta_{e,k}^2$ (since $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ is a unit vector).\par

The $\beta_{e,k}$ pattern constitutes the ``affective fingerprint'' of each named emotion in the pre-verbal meta-axis space. All five meta-axes together explain approximately 22\% of the variance in $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$, while the remaining 78\% is the emotion-specific residual $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$. \par

Several interpretable patterns can be observed in the $\beta_{e,k}$ coefficients. Fear has the most negative value, $\beta_{e,1}$ ($-0.442$), and is clearly situated at the ``calm/objective'' pole of the arousal meta-axis. This is consistent with the view that fear is not a state of high arousal, but rather a state of vigilant inhibition. Surprise has the largest positive value of $\beta_{e,2}$ ($+0.553$) and is located at the ruminative end of the temporal orientation axis. This reflects the retrospective nature of processing unexpected events. Sadness exhibits the largest positive value of $\beta_{e,4}$ ($+0.429$), corresponding to the 'meaningful/fulfilling' end of the existential weight axis. This is consistent with the perception of loss as something possessing personal significance. Expectation exhibits the most negative value, $\beta_{e,2}=-0.319$, and is situated at the practical/future-oriented pole. This contrasts with the retrospective orientation of surprise despite both involving uncertainty. Joy is characterised primarily by $\beta_{e,5} = +0.269$ (release/relief) and $\beta_{e,2} = -0.261$ (forward-looking), reflecting a state of positive resolution rather than turmoil. Trust is divided into $\beta_{e,2} = -0.249$ (future-oriented) and $\beta_{e,4} = -0.211$ (routine/obligatory), suggesting that trust is encoded as practical, low-risk acceptance rather than something of deep personal significance.\par

\subsection*{Connecting to Chapter~\ref{chap:development1_emo_steering}}

We can now state the revised decomposition at the level of the steering vector. Chapter~\ref{chap:development1_emo_steering} used the unit-normalised residual $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ as the primitive emotion-specific direction, namely that:
\[
\Delta_e(\alpha_G,\alpha_R)=\alpha_G\mathbf{g}+\alpha_R\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e.
\tag{Chapter~\ref{chap:development1_emo_steering}}
\]
Chapter~\ref{chap:development2_preverbal_vectors} decomposes $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ into a pre-verbal projection and an orthogonal emotion-specific residual:
\[
\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e = \sum_{k=1}^{K}\beta_{e,k}\,\mathbf{m}_k + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e,
\qquad \beta_{e,k}=\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e^\top\mathbf{m}_k,
\qquad \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e\perp\mathbf{m}_k.
\tag{Chapter~\ref{chap:development2_preverbal_vectors}}
\]
Substituting into the steering formula:
\[
\Delta_e
= \alpha_G\mathbf{g}
  + \underbrace{\alpha_R\!\sum_{k=1}^{K}\beta_{e,k}\,\mathbf{m}_k}_{\text{pre-verbal component}}
  + \underbrace{\alpha_R\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e}_{\text{emotion-specific residual}}.
\tag{Combined}
\]
The practical question is whether the $\alpha_R\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$ term contributes useful signal or noise. This is answered directly in Section~\ref{sec:ch2-label-free-steering}. Condition~\ref{item:condA} steers with the full $\alpha_R\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ (which includes $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$), while Condition~\ref{item:condB} steers with only $\alpha_R\sum_k\beta_{e,k}\mathbf{m}_k$ (which excludes it). Their comparison is therefore a direct empirical test of $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$'s functional role.\par

In sum, what Chapter~\ref{chap:development1_emo_steering} called $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ is not a primitive atom. Named emotions are reinterpreted as \textbf{specific patterns of activation} along label-free pre-verbal dimensions $\mathbf{m}_k$, encoded by the fingerprint $(\beta_{e,1},\dots,\beta_{e,K})$ in Table~\ref{tab:beta_coefficients}.\par

\section{Causal Validation: Local Axis Steering}
\label{sec:ch2-local-steering}

% Exp5 + Exp9: steering along u_{e,1} and m_1.
% local_axis_match results, pole asymmetry.

The internal axes identified by PCA are geometric features of the activation distribution. To establish that they are also causally relevant to generation, we directly steer along them and measure whether the outputs shift in the expected affective direction.\par

\subsection*{Steering formula}

We extend the Chapter~\ref{chap:development1_emo_steering} intervention to include a local axis component:
\[
\Delta_e(\alpha_G,\alpha_R,\beta)
= \alpha_G\mathbf{g}+\alpha_R\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e \pm \beta\,\mathbf{u}_{e,1},
\]
where $+\beta$ steers toward the high pole of the axis and $-\beta$ toward the low pole. In a parallel condition, we substitute the shared meta-axis $\mathbf{m}_1$ for the emotion-specific $\mathbf{u}_{e,1}$.\par

\section{Label-Free Affective Steering}
\label{sec:ch2-label-free-steering}

% Exp10: conditions A (r_e only), B (β_k m_k), C (r_e + m_k).
% B ≥ A in quality; C < B (ε_e is noise).
% Practical implication: steering with Σ β_{e,k} m_k is sufficient.

The Combined formula in Section~\ref{sec:ch2-reinterpretation} isolates a specific empirical question. Is $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$ (the 78\% of $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ that cannot be expressed via $\mathbf{m}_k$) functionally useful for steering, or is it inert noise? This experiment is designed to answer that question directly by constructing three teering conditions that differ only in whether and how $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$ is included.\par

\subsection*{Three steering conditions}

We compare three steering conditions using the same coefficient magnitudes:

\begin{enumerate}[
  label=\textbf{\Alph*:},
  ref=\Alph*
]
  \item \label{item:condA} $\Delta = \alpha_G\mathbf{g}+\alpha_R\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ \par
    Since $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e = \sum_k\beta_{e,k}\mathbf{m}_k + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$, this steers with \textbf{both} the pre-verbal projection and $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$. This is equivalent to the Chapter~\ref{chap:development1_emo_steering} intervention.
  \item \label{item:condB} $\Delta = \alpha_G\mathbf{g}+\sum_k\tilde{\beta}_{e,k}\,\mathbf{m}_k$  where $\tilde{\beta}_{e,k}=\alpha_R\beta_{e,k}=\alpha_R(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e^\top\mathbf{m}_k)$\par
    This steers with only the $\mathbf{m}_k$-projection of $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$, \textbf{explicitly excluding} $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$. The difference A$-$B therefore isolates the causal contribution of $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$.
  \item \label{item:condC} $\Delta = \alpha_G\mathbf{g}+\alpha_R\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e+\sum_k\tilde{\beta}_{e,k}\,\mathbf{m}_k$\par
    which adds the meta-axis projection on top of the full named prototype. Since $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ already contains the $\mathbf{m}_k$ component, this condition double-weights the pre-verbal projection relative to $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$.
\end{enumerate}

This results support the refined view of emotion representation, that named emotion labels function as convenient short-hands for specific activation \textbf{patterns} in the pre-verbal meta-axis space. The $\beta_{e,k}$ values in Table~\ref{tab:beta_coefficients} are the core content of the emotion label. Also, the named prototype $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$ is largely recoverable from these projections, and the residual $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$ is mostly non-functional for generation. Steering with $\sum_k\beta_{e,k}\mathbf{m}_k$ is therefore not only theoretically cleaner but also empirically superior in terms of output quality.\par

\section{Limitations and Transition}
\label{sec:ch2-limitations}

The experiments in this chapter revealed that the emotion-specific residual $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$, derived in Chapter~\ref{chap:development1_emo_steering}, possesses structured internal geometric properties. Its principal variance is organised along a common meta-axis $\mathbf{m}_k$, which has a label-independent interpretation that predates verbalisation. The decomposition formula $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e = \sum_k\beta_{e,k}\mathbf{m}_k + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$ extends the equation from Chapter 1 into a three-tiered hierarchy: shared affective components ($\mathbf{g}$), pre-linguistic affective dimensions ($\mathbf{m}_k$), and emotion-specific small residuals ($\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$).\par

The experiments in this chapter revealed that the emotion-specific residuals $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$, derived in Chapter~\ref{chap:development1_emo_steering}, possess structured internal geometric properties. Their principal variance is organised along a common meta-axis $\mathbf{m}_k$, for which a label-independent interpretation exists prior to verbalisation. The decomposition formula $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e = \sum_k\beta_{e,k}\mathbf{m}_k + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$ extends the equation from Chapter~\ref{chap:development1_emo_steering} into a three-tier hierarchical structure:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Shared emotional components ($\mathbf{g}$)
  \item Pre-verbal emotional dimensions ($\mathbf{m}_k$), and
  \item Emotion-specific small residuals ($\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$).
\end{enumerate}

These conclusions have several limitations. \par

Firstly, the projection onto the meta-axis explains only approximately 22\% of the variance in $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_e$, with the remaining 78\% accounted for by $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$. The label-free steering experiment (Section~\ref{sec:ch2-label-free-steering}) provides empirical evidence that, for most emotions, $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$ contributes as noise rather than signal; however, this does not imply that $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$ is semantically empty. $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$ may encode relevant emotion-specific information under different generative conditions, at different coefficient scales, or with regard to specific emotions (particularly surprise, which is the only case where Condition A outperforms Condition B). The current evidence merely indicates that $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$ is functionally impotent ``under the tested conditions''; it does not demonstrate that it is inherently meaningless.\par

Secondly, the LLM judgement labels for $\mathbf{m}_k$ are assigned a ``moderate'' level of contamination, reflecting the inherent difficulty of generalisation across emotions. Although this interpretation is plausible, it has not been independently verified by human evaluators or psychometric tools. We also need to note that the meta-axes $\mathbf{m}_4$ and $\mathbf{m}_5$ are unstable (inter-method agreement $< 0.3$) and are included only in the calculation of EVR. They are not used as interpretable axes.\par

Thirdly, this evidence is based on a single model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) and a single layer depth (13 layers). Further investigation is required regarding the generalisability to other models and layer depths within the meta-axis structure.  \par

These limitations provide the motivation for the next chapter. The label-free $\mathbf{m}_k$ axes provide a theoretical foundation for our Emotion Engine user interface that is not bound by fixed emotion labels. In the next chapter, we consider how such pre-verbal representations can be made operational in practical user interfaces, and whether the separation of emotionalisation vector ($\mathbf{g}$), emotion texture ($\mathbf{m}_k$), and emotion-specific identity ($\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_e$) can be transformed into meaningful and controllable operational elements for users.\par
